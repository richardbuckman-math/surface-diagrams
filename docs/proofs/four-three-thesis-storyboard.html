Baykur–Korkmaz (4,3): a graphical derivation

Richard Buckman’s thesis, §4.3, Figures 4.5–4.9, already lays this derivation out as horizontal transitions between vertical factorizations. Scroll sideways to follow those original diagrams. Open a figure for full-size inspection.

This is a visual source guide to the Baykur–Korkmaz relation. Open the three checked Figure 4.5 columns for exact five-strand factors and a five-arc disk cut complex after each row. The Xiao (4,3) lab uses a different seven-factor seed.

Stage 1 of 5

1 · Reduced daisy and cyclic permutation

Three factorizations of the exterior boundary twist. Green curves with exponent 2 are inverse squared twists. All three columns now have an exact cut-complex preview.

Thesis Figure 4.5, printed page 34.

2 · Rearrange the marked points

Drag the first and third marked points around the second. This is a change of the planar chart, not an extra factor.

Thesis Figure 4.6, printed page 34.

3 · Square the exterior twist; use half lantern twice

Concatenate two factorizations, commute and cycle factors, then replace two positive/negative-twist pairs by pairs of half twists. Inspect the checked concatenation, rotation, and first combination with cut complexes.

Thesis Figure 4.7, printed page 35.

4 · Hurwitz moves make the odd twists liftable

Follow the five columns left to right. The arrows group several adjacent Hurwitz moves; the last column has only liftable factors.

Thesis Figure 4.8, printed page 36.

5 · Replace points by boundaries and redraw on the sphere

Replace the third and fourth marked points by inner boundaries, include their boundary-twist corrections, and put the exterior boundary on the right of the sphere.
